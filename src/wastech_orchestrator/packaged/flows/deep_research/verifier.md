Verify the report this run will publish against its evidence.{?synthesis_path} It is at `{synthesis_path}` — the deliverable itself, as the writing node produced it.{/synthesis_path} Your job is to judge whether each claim is actually **supported** by what it cites, whether the conclusions follow, and whether anything a full audit of the declared scope should have found is missing from it. The deterministic citation check that ran before you resolves **locations, never claims** — a real snippet at a real line can still carry a fabricated assertion, and that gap is exactly what you are here to close.

{?checks_path}Its per-entry verdicts are at `{checks_path}`; read them first, because they tell you which citations are already suspect. A `weak` entry has its snippet somewhere other than the cited line, so the location is mis-attributed even though the quote is real. An `uncheckable` entry was never checked at all — an external url, or an entry carrying no snippet. Both are yours to resolve: confirm the real location, or fetch the source. Any you cannot resolve is a finding.

That file is also your **scope**: it carries one entry per source in the report's citation manifest, so it tells you how many citations exist — including any the report chose not to surface in its prose. Work the whole list.{/checks_path}

Verify **every** entry, and open the citation manifest itself for the claim each entry is there to support{?checks_path} — `{checks_path}` names the manifest it validated in `manifest_path`, relative to `{repo}`{/checks_path}. A citation you never opened is not verified, and the report's coverage claim must not be left standing on it: name the entry ids you did not reach as a `low` finding against your own pass. Do not let the report's inline prose set your scope — that is how a citation the report preferred not to discuss goes unexamined.

An **external** source (an entry with a `url`) is **fetched**, never recalled: retrieve it with the web tool this node has been granted and check the quoted text against what the page actually says. A fetch that fails, a page that has moved, or a quote you cannot find on it is a finding — an external citation nobody can reach is not evidence. If you have no working web tool, say so and report the entry as unresolved; "confirmed" from memory is the one answer that is never acceptable here.

For a **plan-vs-implementation audit**, a claim usually asserts that shipped code diverges from — or falls short of — the plan of record. Verify _both_ sides against `{repo}`:

- The **code** side: open the cited `path:line` and confirm it really says what the finding claims, in the way the finding claims.
- The **plan** side: open the cited plan document (requirement, decision, invariant, or roadmap entry) and confirm the standard the finding measures against is stated there and read correctly — not paraphrased into something stronger.

Watch for **over**-claiming: severity inflated beyond what the evidence supports; a "gap" that a later unit or a recorded deferral actually closes or intentionally scopes out; and a suspected issue presented as confirmed.

Then watch for **under**-claiming, which nothing above can catch: what should a full audit of this declared scope have found that is absent here? Name a specific subsystem **and** the specific property that was never checked — "the installed command-line entry point is graded walked, but no invocation is traced past its declaration in the manifest" is a finding; "coverage could be deeper" is not one, and do not file it. A carefully hedged report is not thereby a safe one: a "needs confirmation" tier and a paragraph explaining why its severities are not inflated are wording, not evidence, and a report that examined a fifth of its scope can be entirely accurate and still unsafe to act on.

Read only; do not edit. Return findings in the output schema, each with an honest `severity` (blocking / critical / high / medium / low) reflecting how badly the claim is unsupported, misstated, or misclassified. You do not author the verdict — the flow decides which severities force another round, so do not inflate or downplay to force an outcome. File everything you find at its true severity: a finding below the gate is not discarded, it is carried to the operator in the run summary and the pull-request body. This is a non-blocking pass, so an unresolved concern belongs in `findings`, never in prose — and a spent rework budget means the flow accepts and continues rather than parking the task. A report whose claims all hold up returns an empty `findings` array.
